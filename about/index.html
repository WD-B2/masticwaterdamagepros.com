About Mastic Water Damage Pros.

Scope and price explained before work starts.

Call Now.

Mastic Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration company with its address at 1708 Montauk Highway in Mastic, NY. We answer a 24/7 line and work in the hamlets of the Town of Brookhaven around Mastic.

Mastic is a hamlet and census-designated place, not an incorporated city. Its 2020 Census population was 15,404 across 4,792 housing units, and most calls come from single family homes.

How We Work.

Before any work starts, we explain the scope and the price. That means which rooms are affected, what will be removed, what equipment will run and what factors could change the total. You decide with the full picture in front of you.

We are licensed and insured. We do not publish a license number on this website, so ask for the details when you call.

Standards We Follow.

The industry publishes two standards that shape this work. The ANSI/IICRC S500 describes professional water damage restoration, including water categories 1 to 3, and the S520 covers mold remediation. We name them here as published references that guide the work, not as a claim about any certification.

For mold, New York Labor Law Article 32 applies to projects over 10 square feet. The assessor and the remediator must be separate on the same property, and the state Department of Labor offers a licensed contractor search tool. Our mold remediation page explains the steps.

Where We Work.

Our crew covers Mastic and nearby communities such as Brookhaven, Center Moriches, Manorville, Upton and East Moriches.

You can see every community on the service area page. If your street is not listed, call and tell us the address.

What To Do Before a Crew Arrives.

We ask where the water came from, how long it has been there and which rooms are affected. We tell you which first steps are safe to take yourself, such as shutting off a supply valve or moving small items.

We then explain how the work will go, and we give timing when we know it. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

What Affects the Scope and Price.

No two water losses are alike, so we do not quote from a table. The main factors are how much water there is, how long it has been there, which materials it reached and whether the source was clean or contaminated.

Access matters too. A wall cavity that must be opened, a floor that must be lifted or a space under a slab changes the work. We walk through these factors with you before anything starts.

Why the Hamlet Setting Matters.

Mastic sits on the south shore of Long Island, close to the Forge River and Moriches Bay. After Tropical Storm Irene in 2011, the National Weather Service recorded flooding up to 4 feet within 2,000 feet inland from Moriches Bay in neighboring Mastic Beach.

Most homes here are on private septic systems. Suffolk County is about 74 percent unsewered, and the Forge River Watershed Sewer District serves roughly 1,890 homes in the Mastic and Shirley area. We plan for both situations.

Insurance and Records.

The New York Department of Financial Services notes that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. Water back-up and sewage coverage is usually an endorsement, and a denial must cite the policy language.

We photograph the damage, log moisture readings and keep a written scope. Your insurer decides coverage, and these records give it a clear file to work from.

Residential and Commercial Work.

Most of our calls are for single family homes, but we also work in shops, offices and rental buildings. Commercial jobs add questions about tenants, access and downtime, which our commercial water damage restoration page covers.

For homes, our water extraction and structural drying pages show what each stage looks like.

Mold and Water Together.

Water that sits for days can lead to mold, which is why drying speed matters. The EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit growth.

If mold is already present, we explain the New York process before anyone cuts into a wall. That includes the licensed assessor, the written protocol and the separate licensed remediator.

We also tell you what you can do yourself. Small, clearly surface level cases may not need a contractor, and the state Department of Labor does not require homeowners to clean up mold on their own property.
