Shirley is a hamlet and census-designated place in the Town of Brookhaven, ZIP 11967, next to Mastic. It was once called Tangiers, and the post office also accepts the names Smith Point and East Yaphank for parts of the area.

The 2020 Census counted 26,360 people and 8,782 housing units here, with 5.9 percent vacant. The hamlet covers about 12 square miles, including roughly 0.85 square miles of water.

The Mastic line is answered 24/7, so a call from a Shirley address is picked up at any hour. Give us the cross street and the source of the water, and we explain the first steps.

Where Shirley Sits.

The Wertheim National Wildlife Refuge covers most of western Shirley, so much of the western side is protected land rather than housing. The Smith Point Bridge, the second of two road crossings from Long Island to Fire Island, lands in the hamlet.

Three school districts serve Shirley. William Floyd covers the southern, eastern and central parts, South Country covers the west, and Longwood covers the north. The Mastic Shirley station on the Long Island Rail Road and the 7th Precinct of the Suffolk County Police Department are other useful landmarks for finding an address.

Elevation, Rain and Storm Water.

The listed elevation is about 52 feet, a single figure for a hamlet of roughly 12 square miles. Streets near marsh or bay edges can sit lower than that number suggests.

County officials, quoted by South Shore Press, tied the Sandy breaches of 2012 to widespread flooding across the Mastic-Shirley peninsula. That is regional evidence, not a statement about any one street in Shirley.

If Water Is Still Spreading in Shirley.

Stop the source.

Close the main valve or the fixture shutoff if you can reach it safely. If you cannot find it, call and we will help.

Keep clear of electrical hazards.

Do not stand in water near outlets, cords or the panel. Call the utility or emergency services for immediate dangers.

Lift valuables off the floor.

Move rugs, shoes, papers and small electronics to a dry shelf when it is safe to do so.

Photograph the damage.

Take wide and close photos before anything is moved. They help when you talk to your insurer.

Sewers, Septic and Backups.

The treatment plant for the Forge River Watershed Sewer District is in Shirley, and the district serves about 1,890 homes in the Mastic and Shirley area. Suffolk County as a whole is about 74 percent unsewered, so many homes rely on septic systems or cesspools.

Ask your district or the Town whether your street is connected. A backup is a different job from a clean water leak, and sewage backup cleanup explains the protective steps involved.

What Extraction and Drying Involve.

Pumps and wet vacuums remove standing water, then air movers and dehumidifiers dry what is left. A moisture meter confirms when framing and subfloor are back to normal.

Our page on water extraction shows the equipment. Drying matters because the EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.

When Mold Is a Concern.

If you see growth after a leak, stop and read how New York handles it. The state Department of Labor says its mold law applies to projects over 10 square feet and that homeowners are not required to clean up mold.

The assessor and the remediator must be separate on one property. Mold remediation explains the steps and the licensed contractor search tool.
