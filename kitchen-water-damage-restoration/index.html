Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Mastic, NY.

Kitchens combine plumbing, appliances, cabinets and flooring in one room. Here is how water damage there is dried and repaired.

Kitchens are the busiest water room in the house. A sink supply line, a dishwasher hose and an ice maker line all sit within a few feet of cabinets and flooring, so a leak soaks several materials at once.

If water is running, close the shutoff valve under the sink or at the main, and turn off the breaker for any outlet near the water. Take photos before pulling anything out of cabinets.

This page explains where kitchen leaks begin, how cabinets and floors are dried, and what repair work follows.

Many kitchen losses start with an appliance leak and end at the floor, so see our hardwood floor page if your boards have cupped.

Where Kitchen Leaks Begin.

Sink supply and drain lines.

Flexible supply lines and P-traps under the sink loosen, crack or corrode.

Dishwasher connections.

A loose inlet or a worn door gasket leaks into the cabinet base.

Refrigerator water lines.

Ice maker and dispenser tubing fail behind the unit. See appliance leak water damage.

Ceiling and wall leaks.

A bathroom or roof leak above shows up as stains in a kitchen ceiling.

What Gets Wet.

Water follows gravity. It pools under the sink, runs through the cabinet floor and into the subfloor. It travels along the toe kick and into the base of the wall. Under laminate and vinyl, it spreads out of sight.

Cabinets made of particleboard swell and fail when soaked, while solid wood and plywood often dry. A technician checks each cabinet base with a meter and explains which parts can be saved.

How Drying Works Around Cabinets.

1

Stop the water and remove contents.

Items are cleared from the wet cabinets and surfaces are photographed.

2

Remove toe kicks and open the base.

Openings allow air to reach the cabinet floor and wall.

3

Dry in place when possible.

Small drilled holes or removed panels let drying equipment push air behind the cabinet.

4

Replace what cannot dry.

Swollen particleboard or saturated drywall is removed and replaced.

Flooring Under a Kitchen.

Tile and vinyl often hold up better than wood, but water can sit under them. Hardwood may cup, and laminate may swell at the edges. See hardwood floor water damage repair for how wood floors are dried.

Kitchen floors are often the same height as the rest of the house, so water spreads to adjoining rooms. Moisture mapping shows how far it went.

Repair After Drying.

Once readings are normal, repair work includes drywall, baseboards, flooring and sometimes cabinet components. Mitigation and repair are separate stages, and the scope for each is explained before work starts.

Plumbing repair is done by a licensed plumber. We dry the structure and restore the finishes that were removed.

Countertops, Appliances and Contents in a Wet Kitchen.

A kitchen holds heavy items on top of fragile materials. Stone countertops may need to be detached to dry the cabinets beneath, and laminate counters with particleboard cores swell at the edges when water reaches them from underneath.

Appliances such as dishwashers and refrigerators sit on the floor and may need to be pulled out to reach the wet area. Disconnect them carefully, and photograph the model and serial labels. Food that touched flood or sewage water should be discarded.

Take photos of cabinet interiors before emptying them, and keep a list of pots, small appliances and pantry items that were affected. A written inventory helps your insurer and speeds up the claim.

What Changes the Scope and Cost.

Cabinet material.

Particleboard is often replaced, and plywood and solid wood can be dried.

Flooring.

Hardwood and laminate are more sensitive than tile.

Extent of travel.

Water that moved into the next room adds work.

Countertops.

Stone and laminate countertops may need to be detached to dry cabinets.

What To Do Before a Crew Arrives.

  • Shut off the water supply if you can reach the valve safely.
  • Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances while standing in water.
  • Do not enter a room with a sagging or bulging ceiling.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything.
  • Move small valuables and papers to a dry shelf, then call the line.

Reference Standards.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it defines water categories 1 to 3 by how contaminated the source is. We cite it as a reference for Kitchen Water Damage Restoration and not as a claim about any certification. The EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.
