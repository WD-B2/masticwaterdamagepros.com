Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage in Mastic, NY.

Water leaking beneath or inside a concrete slab shows up as warm floors, a spinning water meter or damp flooring. Here is what to do.

Call Now.

A slab leak is a 24/7 emergency when water is coming up through the floor, and the Mastic line answers at all hours. Close the main valve and check whether the water meter still moves with everything off. If it does, you likely have a leak in a line.

Take photos of wet flooring and any bubbling, and keep people off wet areas. Do not drill or cut the slab yourself.

Slab leaks are hard to diagnose because the pipe is hidden. This page explains the symptoms, who fixes what, and how the water damage is handled.

Once the leak is found, structural drying takes over, and the same moisture can affect a crawl space in homes built differently.

Why Is My Floor Warm and My Water Bill High?

Warm spots on the floor.

Hot water lines under the slab leak and heat the concrete above them.

Sound of running water.

You hear hissing or running water when all fixtures are off.

Rising water bill.

Usage jumps without a change in habits. Compare bills against last season.

Damp flooring or baseboards.

Carpet or wood near the leak stays wet. Wood may cup and baseboards may swell.

Low water pressure.

Pressure drops at fixtures when a line is leaking underground.

Any one of these signs can have other causes, and together they point toward a leak under the floor. A licensed plumber confirms the leak with listening and pressure equipment.

Who Does What.

A leak detection plumber finds the line and repairs or reroutes it. We handle the water damage that results. That includes extraction, drying of flooring, subfloor and walls, and documentation for your insurer.

If the repair needs breaking the slab, plan for dust and noise and coordinate timing with your plumber so drying begins promptly afterward.

Damage From Slab Leaks.

Water moves through gaps in the slab and into carpet, pad, hardwood and baseboards. It can wick up drywall from the bottom and cause a musty smell long before a stain appears.

Long running leaks may soften soil under the slab. That is a plumbing and structural question for professionals. See structural drying and dehumidification for how walls and floors are dried after the repair.

How We Dry After the Repair.

1

Stop the leak.

The plumber repairs the line first.

2

Extract water.

Surface water and wet padding are removed.

3

Lift and dry flooring.

Flooring that traps moisture is lifted or opened. Mats and air movers dry the slab surface.

4

Verify dryness.

Moisture readings on walls and floors confirm the structure is back to normal.

Insurance Notes.

Policies differ on what is covered when a slab leak is found. Some pay for resulting water damage and exclude the cost of reaching the pipe. Your policy decides, and your insurer makes the call.

Keep the plumber report, photographs and your water bills. Those records show when the leak began.

Questions To Ask a Leak Detection Plumber.

A plumber who locates slab leaks uses listening devices, pressure testing and sometimes thermal or acoustic equipment. Ask how they will find the line, and whether the repair involves breaking the slab or rerouting the pipe through walls or the attic.

Ask for a written finding that states where the leak was and what pipe failed. Your insurer may need it, and so do we when we plan drying. Ask how long the repair will take, since drying can begin once the line is fixed and the area is accessible.

If the leak is in a hot water line, the water heater may be involved. See water heater leak cleanup for how that source is handled.

What Changes the Scope and Cost.

Flooring type.

Hardwood and laminate are harder to save than tile.

Duration.

A slow leak over months affects more material.

Area.

The longer the line, the larger the wet zone.

Access.

Moving furniture and lifting floors add time.

What To Do Before a Crew Arrives.

  • Shut off the water supply if you can reach the valve safely.
  • Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances while standing in water.
  • Do not enter a room with a sagging or bulging ceiling.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything.
  • Move small valuables and papers to a dry shelf, then call the line.

Reference Standards.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it defines water categories 1 to 3 by how contaminated the source is. We cite it as a reference for Slab Leak Water Damage and not as a claim about any certification. The EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.
