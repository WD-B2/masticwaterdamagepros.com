Brookhaven is both the name of a town and of a hamlet, and the two are easy to confuse. Mastic sits inside the Town of Brookhaven, while this page covers the smaller hamlet of Brookhaven, ZIP 11719, about four miles west southwest of us by straight line.

The Mastic line runs around the clock, so a call from a Brookhaven address is answered at any hour. Tell us the cross street and where the water is, and we explain the first steps.

The 2020 Census counted 3,330 people in the hamlet, down slightly from 3,451 in 2010, spread across about 5.94 square miles. It is directly governed by the Town of Brookhaven, which matters for permits.

The Neighborhood Around Beaver Dam Road.

The Brookhaven Free Library stands at Beaver Dam Road and Library Lane, and the South Country Central School District serves the hamlet. Those landmarks anchor most of the homes we would reach from Montauk Highway and the roads that feed it.

The hamlet sits about 10 feet above sea level, lower than Mastic at about 30 feet. Creek and bay side streets here have less ground between a rising tide and a floor, so water reaches homes with less warning.

Emergency Water Removal for Brookhaven Properties at Any Hour.

Water does not wait for business hours, and neither does the Mastic line. A burst supply line at 2 a.m. gets the same questions as one at noon. Where is the water, what is the source, and is the power on.

For standing water we start with water extraction, then drying. When the source is a pipe, our guidance on burst pipe water damage explains what to shut off and photograph first.

Water History Near the Creek and Bay.

The Beaver Dam Creek jetty in the hamlet was rebuilt in 2017 after damage from Superstorm Sandy in 2012. That is a reminder that storm surge reached this shoreline, and homes near creeks and canals face a different risk from homes inland.

For flood events, flood damage restoration explains how contaminated water is handled. Flood zones are specific to each address, so use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and do not rely on a general statement about the hamlet.

Permits, Repairs and the Town.

The Town of Brookhaven Division of Building administers the New York State Uniform Fire Prevention and Building Code. Whether a repair needs a permit depends on the work, so ask the building division before you commit to a scope.

Our scope separates mitigation from reconstruction, so you can see which parts are drying and which are rebuilding.

Low Ground Near the Creek: What To Check.

Homes close to Beaver Dam Creek and the canals sit on lower ground than most of Mastic. After heavy rain or a storm tide, look for water in crawl spaces, around the water heater and at the base of exterior walls, because those are the places it shows first.

Look for silt lines, a damp smell and warped baseboards. If you find them, take photos and call. Crawl space water damage explains how those areas are pumped and dried.

Because the hamlet is small and governed directly by the Town, many neighbors know each other, and a quick check on an older neighbor after a storm can catch a leak early. That habit of looking out for each other is useful when a pipe fails while someone is away.

Reference Standards.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it defines water categories 1 to 3 by how contaminated the source is. We cite it as a reference for water damage in Brookhaven and not as a claim about any certification. The EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.
