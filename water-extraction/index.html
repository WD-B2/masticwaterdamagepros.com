Standing water is an emergency from the first minute, and our line in Mastic answers 24/7. While you wait for a call back, shut off the water source if you can reach it safely and keep everyone out of rooms where outlets, cords or appliances are in the water.

Move small valuables, shoes and paper goods to a dry shelf. Do not run a household vacuum, and do not walk through deep water to reach a breaker panel.

Water extraction is the first physical step of restoration. Pumps and wet vacuums pull bulk water out of carpet, padding and flooring before it wicks into drywall, framing and subfloor. The sections below explain how that works, what changes the scope, and how extraction connects to drying and repair.

What Water Extraction Removes.

Extraction targets free water, meaning water you can see pooled on a floor or held in carpet and padding. Removing it early shortens the whole job because every gallon left behind has to evaporate later, and evaporation is the slow part of drying.

Technicians use submersible pumps for deep water, truck-mounted or portable extractors for carpet, and weighted wands for hard floors and thresholds. On wood flooring and over concrete, specialty mats can pull water out of the board and the joint without lifting the floor.

Extraction does not finish the job on its own. It leaves materials damp, and the remaining moisture is handled by structural drying and dehumidification so that framing and subfloor reach normal readings.

Why Speed Matters in Mastic.

Mastic is a residential hamlet in the Town of Brookhaven with 4,792 housing units in the 2020 Census, and most calls come from finished living space. Finished floors hide water. A wet pad under carpet or a wet subfloor under vinyl can stay saturated for days while the surface looks nearly normal.

The EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth. That window is the reason extraction comes first. If extraction waits until morning, the same loss can reach baseboards, wall cavities and cabinet bases.

A quick check before you call.

Note where the water entered, how far it has spread, and whether it is clear, cloudy or dark. Those three details tell a technician which equipment to bring and whether the job involves contaminated water.

Does the Water Source Change the Plan.

Yes, and it changes it a lot. Clear water from a supply line is handled very differently from water that came up through a floor drain or toilet. If the water is dark, smells of sewage or came from outside, treat it as contaminated and read about sewage backup cleanup before touching anything.

A supply line that is still running is a plumbing problem first. If a pipe has failed, the guidance on burst pipe water damage explains what to shut off and what to photograph before cleanup starts.

What Changes the Scope and Cost.

We do not publish dollar ranges because every extraction job is different. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and these are the factors that move them.

Volume and depth.

A thin film on one floor is a short job. Several inches across a finished lower level needs pumps and more labor.

Water category.

Clean, gray and black water are handled differently, and contaminated water adds protective steps and disposal.

Floor type.

Carpet over pad, floating floors, hardwood and tile each release water at a different rate.

Access.

Stairs, tight crawl spaces and furniture that must be moved all add time.

Reference Standards.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it defines water categories 1 to 3 by how contaminated the source is. We cite it as a reference for Water Extraction and Removal and not as a claim about any certification. The EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.
