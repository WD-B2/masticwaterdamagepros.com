Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Mastic, NY.

Sewage backups carry bacteria and require protective steps. Here is what to do and how cleanup is handled in Mastic homes.

Call Now.

Sewage backup is a 24/7 emergency, and our Mastic line answers every hour. Stop using water in the house, keep children and pets out of the area, and do not touch the water with bare hands.

Open windows for ventilation if the weather allows, and do not run fans that spread contamination into other rooms. Take photos from a safe distance.

Sewage water is a Category 3 loss in the language of the IICRC S500 standard, which is a published water damage standard. This page explains how that changes the cleanup and what a Mastic homeowner should know about local systems.

Backups often come with flood damage after heavy rain or with water in a basement, so the pages work together. Homes in nearby Shirley are covered on their own page.

Why Is Sewage Coming Up in My Tub or Floor Drain?

Toilet bubbles or gurgles.

Air is being pushed back through a partial blockage in the line.

Several fixtures back up at once.

A tub, a floor drain and a toilet all rising points to a main line problem.

Foul odor with wet floors.

Water from a drain at the lowest level carries a sewage smell.

Slow drains after rain.

Heavy rain or a high water table stresses lines and septic systems.

A blockage in the main line, a failed septic component or a municipal problem can all cause backup. A plumber diagnoses the cause. We clean up the contaminated water and dry the structure.

Why Sewage Is a Different Kind of Loss.

The IICRC S500 standard calls water from sanitary sewers Category 3. It contains bacteria and other pathogens. Porous materials that touch it, such as carpet, padding and drywall base, are removed instead of dried in place.

Technicians wear protective equipment, contain the area and use antimicrobial products on hard surfaces. The goal is to remove contaminated material, sanitize what stays and dry the structure.

What Mastic Homeowners Should Know About Local Systems.

Suffolk County officials describe about three quarters of the county as unsewered. Many Mastic homes still use cesspools or septic systems, and a septic failure can cause a backup into a basement or floor drain.

The Forge River Watershed Sewer District was built to serve about 1,890 homes in Mastic and Shirley, with a treatment plant in Shirley. Whether your address is inside the district is a question for Suffolk County and the Town of Brookhaven.

Local note.

Do not assume you are connected to a sewer. If you are unsure, check your property records and ask the county. A plumber or septic contractor can confirm what system serves the house.

Insurance Notes.

The New York Department of Financial Services notes that water back-up and sewage coverage is typically an endorsement on a homeowners policy. It is often excluded when flooding caused the backup. A denial should state a reason tied to the policy language.

Photograph the damage and keep records. We do not decide coverage and we do not name insurers.

After the Cleanup: Odor, Re-Entry and Contents.

Odors can remain after sewage is removed if materials absorbed it. Porous items that cannot be cleaned are removed so the smell does not return. Technicians check wall bases, floor edges and the area around the drain, because those are the places contamination hides.

Do not move back in until the area is cleaned, dried and checked. Washable fabrics may be laundered in hot water, and items that cannot be cleaned are discarded. Photograph anything you throw away so you can list it for your insurer.

If the backup was part of a larger flood, see flood damage restoration. If the source was a failing septic system, a septic contractor must repair it before the area is safe to use again.

What To Do Before a Crew Arrives.

  • Shut off the water supply if you can reach the valve safely.
  • Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances while standing in water.
  • Do not enter a room with a sagging or bulging ceiling.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything.
  • Move small valuables and papers to a dry shelf, then call the line.

Reference Standards.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it defines water categories 1 to 3 by how contaminated the source is. We cite it as a reference for Sewage Backup Cleanup and not as a claim about any certification. The EPA advises drying water damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth.
